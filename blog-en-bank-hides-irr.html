Bank Says 6%... The Reality Is 11.2%

🕐 5 min Axiom Zones axiomzones.com

Why 6% Is Not Really 6%?

When a bank advertises "6% annual interest" it is not technically lying. But it carefully selects what to show you. The 6% is calculated on the full original balance throughout the entire loan term. But your balance decreases with every monthly payment.

APR vs IRR — The Real Difference

APR: What the bank advertises — calculated on the original balance.

True IRR: The actual cost — accounts for the decreasing balance. Always higher.

Golden Rule: The longer the loan term — the wider the gap between APR and true IRR.

Real Example With Numbers

  • Loan: 100,000 x 48 months x APR 6%
  • Monthly payment: 2,348
  • Total you will pay: 112,704
  • True IRR: 11.2% annually — not 6%

5 Tricks Banks Use

1. Monthly payment focus — hides the true total.

2. Term extension — lowers payment but interest doubles.

3. Administrative fees — added to loan and interest charged on them.

4. Mandatory insurance — raises the true cost.

5. Emotional marketing — bypasses rational thinking.
