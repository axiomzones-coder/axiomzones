The 5 Tricks

How Banks Beautify Loans

Understanding these protects your money. Every one of them is used daily.

01
🎭
The Monthly Payment Illusion
They advertise the monthly payment, never the total. A loan that "costs only 1,200/month" sounds affordable — until you multiply by 60 months.
Bank ad: "Only $1,200/month"
Reality: You pay $72,000 for a $50,000 car.
02
📊
The Simple Rate Deception
Banks quote a "flat" or "simple" interest rate — calculated on the original balance. But you pay it even as the balance shrinks, making the true rate (IRR) nearly double.
Bank says: 6% simple
True IRR: 11.2% — almost double.
03
📅
The Long Rope Trap
Extending the term lowers the monthly payment but massively increases total interest. Banks profit more from longer loans — and they know most people focus only on the monthly amount.
5-year term: Total interest $8,000
7-year term: Total interest $14,500 — same loan.
04
🛡️
The Hidden Fees Layer
Processing fees, insurance, life cover, early repayment penalties — all added quietly to the loan amount, turning into interest-bearing additions you never noticed agreeing to.
Loan: $50,000 — but you receive $46,500
$3,500 in fees became part of the interest base.
05
💊
The Emotional Marketing Trigger
"Own your dream car now." "Move into your home today." Banks pair the loan with aspirational language to bypass rational thinking and trigger emotional commitment.
The math: logical
The marketing: emotional — and emotion almost always wins.

FAQ

Common Questions

What is the Beautification Score? ▼
A 0–100 score showing how misleading a loan offer is. 0 = total transparency (rare). 100 = maximum hiding. It's calculated from the gap between advertised APR and true IRR, plus the ratio of extra cost to loan amount.
What is IRR and why does it differ from APR? ▼
IRR (Internal Rate of Return) is calculated using Newton-Raphson iteration — the same method banks use internally. Simple APR applies the rate to the original balance throughout. IRR accounts for the reducing balance, typically resulting in a higher (more honest) number.
When is a loan financially justified? ▼
When: (1) the purchased asset appreciates in value (property, education, business), (2) your investment return exceeds the loan's true IRR, (3) inflation is higher than the loan rate — making future payments cheaper in real terms, or (4) it's an unavoidable necessity. Consumer loans for depreciating assets (phones, cars, furniture) rarely make pure financial sense.
Does Kashf store my financial data? ▼
No. All calculations run entirely in your browser. Nothing is sent to any server. The URL share feature encodes your numbers in the link itself — we never see them. Your financial data is yours alone.
How is Kashf different from a regular loan calculator? ▼
Most calculators show you the monthly payment — confirming what the bank already told you. Kashf shows the Beautification Score (how misleading the offer is), true IRR vs simple APR, the savings alternative (what if you invested instead), inflation impact, debt burden gauge, 3-bank comparison, and a clear verdict on whether the loan makes financial sense.
What is the opportunity cost? ▼
The money you give up by choosing to pay interest instead of invest. If you pay $10,000 in interest over 4 years, and you could have earned 7% per year on that money, your true cost is significantly higher than $10,000. Kashf calculates this precisely.
